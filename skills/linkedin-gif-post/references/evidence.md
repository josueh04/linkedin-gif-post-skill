# Evidence

The rules come from a frame-by-frame study, made in October 2026, of eight high-performing LinkedIn lead-magnet posts and of 192 posts by their authors, plus the first results from posts made with this method. Engagement is measured as comments per 1,000 followers, so that accounts of different sizes compare.

## The eight references

| Ref | Format | Comments per 1k followers | What the media shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | MP4, 8 s | 37.3 | 11 working mini UIs, each proving one principle; a seamless rewind loop |
| B | GIF, 9.8 s | 29.9 | A 36-card system map; the CTA band is the largest type on the canvas |
| C | GIF, 8 s | 17.1 (early) | Notifications landing on a lock screen; frame 1 is the finished state |
| D | GIF, 9 s | 8.2 | A 13-step pipeline built row by row |
| E | GIF, 6.6 s | 6.5 | A scroll through a cheat sheet; 10 MB, small CTA |
| F | MP4, 15 s | 5.6 | A road map; the keyword arrives only at 6 s |
| G | MP4, 19.8 s | 5.4 | Six pillars; the keyword is on screen 97% of the time |
| H | GIF, 8 s | 1.5 (early) | A launch page with a mascot; a seamless ambient loop |

A ninth reference, studied later, set the ambient style:
- six product-style cards (a line chart, a streak grid, bars, a Venn, a list and a curve), at 1080x1350, 15 fps and 10 s;
- only 7% of the canvas moves at any moment, but 73% of the body moves at some point, and the loop never clears.

## Why they work, ranked

1. The media previews the resource as a working system, dense and specific. It is too detailed to read fully in the feed, which makes people want the full thing.
2. Line 1 of the caption names the topic and a narrow job, and the caption's list mirrors the visual.
3. The CTA stays on screen the whole loop.
4. When the offer is a tool, the outcome appears in a native-looking UI.
5. The topic decodes in under a second, through a logo or a name.
6. Loop craft: one motion grammar, a hold long enough to read, and no visible cut.

## Patterns across 60 lead-magnet posts by five of the authors

Each multiple compares a post's comments with the median of the same author's lead-magnet posts.

| Pattern | Comments vs author median | Posts |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword only in the media | 1.23x | 37 |
| Keyword also in the caption | 0.86x | 23 |
| Hook "I built a complete X" | 1.94x | 11 |
| Hook names the tool only | 1.12x | 23 |
| Numbered list in the caption | 1.53x | 25 |
| No numbered list | 1.00x | 35 |
| Caption under 170 words | 2.72x | 6 |
| Caption over 300 words | 0.80x | 13 |
| GIF vs static, same author | 1.9x to 3.4x for three authors, 0.4x for one | |

The share of animated posts among these authors went from 4% in July to 37% in October 2026. Animation alone is no longer novel; quality and the offer now separate posts.

## First result with this method

One post was republished with an animated GIF in place of a static photo, with a new hook. It drew about 5.2 comments per 1k followers in 13 hours; the static version had ended near 2.5. Because the hook also changed, this is one data point, not proof.

LinkedIn kept all 240 frames of that GIF and re-encoded it only slightly (4.15 MB to 4.08 MB).
